Bowen's Reaction Series

In the early 1900s, N.L. Bowen conducted experimental laboratory studies simulating magma cooling paths. He demonstrated that mineral crystallization processes follow a highly reproducible temperature sequence. As a silicate magma mass transitions from higher liquidus states down to solid structural arrays, minerals isolate from residual liquids at targeted thresholds.

The matrix organizes into two concurrent trends: the discontinuous evolutionary path (transforming structural crystalline rings from isolated silica tetrahedra to complex sheet chain arrays) and the continuous trend (exchanging elemental pathways across calcic and sodic plagioclase variations).

Of course! Let's break it down simply 🌋:

Think of magma like liquid soup cooling down. As it cools, certain ingredients freeze into solid crystals before others.

  • First to form (High Heat): Minerals like Olivine crystalize first at very high temperatures.
  • Last to form (Cool Temp): Minerals like Quartz freeze last when the magma is almost entirely cool.
